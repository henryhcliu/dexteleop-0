DexTeleop-0: Force-Aware Bimanual Dexterous Teleoperation with Ego-Centric Perception towards Shared Autonomy

Haichao Liu1, Yuyao Jiang1, Hyunsun Park2, Yuanjiang Xue1, Mingze Sun1, Ziwei Liu1, Ziwei Wang1,†

1 Nanyang Technological University, Singapore

2 OOJU, USA · † Corresponding author

Paper arXiv

Robot-side fingertip feedback refines retargeted hand motion while the operator supplies the manipulation strategy.

01 / Overview

Preserve human intent. Refine the contact.

Egocentric tracking makes dexterous teleoperation accessible, but matching human motion does not directly regulate robot contact forces. Differences in hand geometry and tracking accuracy can leave contact errors that are difficult to correct through visual feedback alone.

DexTeleop-0 adds a local feedback loop around the operator’s retargeted hand commands. Estimated contact positions and fingertip forces inform small joint corrections during contact, while arm motion remains nominal.

Shared autonomy here means autonomous robot-side contact assistance. The operator receives visual feedback; the interface does not reflect forces back to the human.

  1. 01

    Contact-aware motion correction

    A local stiffness–Jacobian model predicts how hand-joint changes affect individual fingertip forces.

  2. 02

    Bounded residual optimization

    Per-contact vector force tracking and soft moment regularization refine each hand’s target independently.

  3. 03

    Complementary evaluation protocols

    Controlled simulation, fixed-input hardware replay, a seven-operator study, and manual-disturbance traces examine different aspects of assistance.

02 / Method

A contact-feedback loop around teleoperation.

The human supplies nominal motion through a Meta Quest 3 headset. The controller changes hand targets during contact, with a separate optimization for each hand.

  1. 01

    Track & retarget

    Wrist poses drive arm IK. Vector-based retargeting maps tracked hand keypoints to nominal robot-hand targets.

  2. 02

    Estimate contacts

    A hand-specific adapter supplies valid contact positions, force vectors, and confidence in the hand-base frame.

  3. 03

    Optimize residuals

    A bounded quadratic program tracks per-contact forces and penalizes predicted contact moment outside a tolerance.

  4. 04

    Execute at 30 Hz

    Add the residual to the nominal hand target, respect joint limits, and repeat with fresh fingertip feedback.

Residuals apply to hand joints. Arm commands remain nominal.

Local contact model

Track individual forces, then coordinate their moments.

The kinematic Jacobian predicts contact displacement from a joint residual. A local stiffness approximation maps that displacement to a predicted force change. Separate vector errors retain each contact’s load: opposing forces can cancel in the resultant while individual forces remain large.

The moment term is a soft regularizer about the current contact centroid. It does not impose hard equilibrium or model every external load. The formulation uses a moment tolerance of 10 N·m (10 Nest·m for Wuji), with at least two valid contacts.

Direct feedback

Sharpa Wave 22 DoF per hand

Tactile contact locations, finger geometry, and force-vector channels supply the contact state.

Estimated feedback

Wuji Hand 2 20 joints per hand

Joint position, velocity, and current inform a quasi-static, normal-force estimate. Its empirical Nest values are distinct from calibrated force measurements.

Inspect contact-force and manual-disturbance analyses

A small resultant can hide large individual loads.

These traces share recorded nominal commands and elapsed time in a Ball Assembly replay. Opposing contact forces can cancel in the resultant; separate per-contact tracking retains the individual errors.

Manual disturbance provides a descriptive contact test.

In the illustrated watermelon-model grasp, No Residual and DexForce contacts decay near zero at 12–14 s; DexTeleop-0 retains thumb and middle contact until about 21 s. Manual disturbance amplitude and timing vary, so these traces do not quantify calibrated disturbance rejection.

Scope & limitations

Local correction depends on the contact model.

The first-order model depends on contact estimates, stiffness approximations, and contacts remaining approximately fixed over an update. Object weight and unmeasured environmental contacts are not fully modeled. Each hand is optimized independently; the soft moment penalty acts outside its configured tolerance. The results support task-dependent contact assistance rather than universal equilibrium, safety, or speed guarantees.
